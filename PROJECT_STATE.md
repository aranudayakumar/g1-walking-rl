- **Four documented alternatives exist relative to `exp0017`'s Pareto surface, not superseded by ADR-011, not adopted as canonical:**
  - `exp0018_air_time_v3` (`experiments/runs/exp0018_air_time_v3/best_checkpoint_2M.zip`): longest verified stride (60ms median swing, tripling the original 20ms baseline), but `dist_ratio` down 23% and heading control worse (-28.2 deg/10s controlled test) than `exp0017`.   - `exp0019_heading_hold` (`experiments/runs/exp0019_heading_hold/best_checkpoint_8M.zip`): still holds the single best *controlled-heading* measurement in the project (-7.5 deg/10s, vs. the new canonical's -14.1 deg/10s) -- the one axis `exp0026` does not win outright -- but `exp0026` beats it on tracking, full-eval heading, and falls.
  - `exp0029_dr_on_exp0026` (`experiments/runs/exp0029_dr_on_exp0026/best_checkpoint_6M.zip`): a robustness axis rather than a gait-shape axis -- domain randomization (EXP-0014/15's recipe) re-trained on top of the canonical `exp0026` reward via warm-start fine-tuning. Falls under randomized physics vs. `exp0026` evaluated on the same randomized conditions drop 14/20 -> 4/20 (71% relative reduction) and survival nearly doubles (10.2s -> 18.8s), at a real clean-physics cost (`dist_ratio` 0.918 -> 0.643) that still beats the *old* canonical `exp0017`'s own clean-physics number. Supersedes `exp0021_dr_on_canonical` (built on the now-superseded `exp0017`) for this purpose; `exp0021`/`exp0015` kept for history.
  - `exp0031_heading_on_exp0026_lowlr` (`experiments/runs/exp0031_heading_on_exp0026_lowlr/best_checkpoint_3M.zip`): best raw distance-coverage/`dist_ratio` in the project (1.099), a real verified stride matching the project's best, and stable 0/20 falls -- reached by fine-tuning `heading_deviation_penalty` onto `exp0026` at a much lower LR (1e-5) than usual, which stabilized falls where two weight-only attempts (EXP-0027/0028) failed. Trades away tracking precision (`vel_err` 0.036 -> 0.061) for that extra distance coverage; did not close the controlled-heading gap it originally targeted.
- **How the stepping-and-heading investigation unfolded (EXP-0010 through EXP-0020)**: two from-scratch attempts to reward longer swing phases both failed (skating exploit; then 100% falls fixing that exploit, F-0004). Warm-start fine-tuning (`training/train.py --init-from`/`--init-learning-rate`, a new capability) from an established policy at a low learning rate fixed this -- swing duration doubled with no exploit (EXP-0016), then tripled with continued budget reaching 0/20 falls (EXP-0017). Pushing the same lever harder (EXP-0018) revealed a Pareto trade-off: longer strides cost heading control. Measuring *why* (instantaneous rotation-rate-squared can't distinguish a small steady drift bias from harmless zero-mean oscillation) motivated a better-targeted `heading_deviation_penalty` term (penalizes net drift from episode-start heading directly), which recovered and exceeded the original heading quality while keeping the longer strides (EXP-0019) -- though still short of canonical's tracking precision. A budget-extension test (EXP-0020) confirmed this specific lineage has genuinely plateaued, not simply undertrained.
- **A secondary, twice-confirmed finding**: fine-tuning runs (EXP-0016/0017, 15M combined timesteps) show smooth, steadily-improving training curves with none of the violent climb-crash-recover oscillation that characterizes every from-scratch run in this project (F-0002). Suggestive (not proven as universal) that low-LR fine-tuning avoids that instability pattern, not just exploit-finding.
- **User-reported concern ("does it walk straight / take steps?") led to a new diagnostic** (`diagnostics/gait_diagnostic.py`) that found and enabled fixing both the straight-line problem (ADR-005, EXP-0011) and the stepping-quality problem (ADR-007/ADR-008/ADR-010, EXP-0016/0017/0019) -- none of these were visible to the aggregate metrics (reward, survival, `dist_ratio`) used before it existed.
- **Domain randomization (Phase 8) is implemented, verified, and shows a genuine (modest) robustness gain**, kept as a separate alternative to the canonical baseline (built on the pre-EXP-0016 reward; not yet re-run on top of the stepping-quality fix). `envs/domain_randomization.py` randomizes friction/mass/motor-strength per episode and applies periodic pushes; disabled by default (zero effect on any prior config, verified by test). EXP-0015 (20M timesteps) confirmed a policy trained with randomization survives longer and falls less under randomized physics than a no-randomization control, at the cost of less precise tracking. Available at `experiments/runs/exp0015_domain_randomization_long/best_checkpoint_20M.zip`.
- What actually fixed the earlier residual falls (superseded but kept for history): more training, not reward/PPO-config changes -- see `docs/DECISIONS.md` ADR-004, `docs/FAILURE_ANALYSIS.md` F-0002.
- Standing practices established by this project, all still in effect: checkpoint selection via evaluation across the whole trend (`evaluation/evaluate_checkpoints.py`), always checking `dist_ratio` not just reward/survival (ADR-003), checking heading drift and swing-time distribution directly (`mean_abs_yaw_drift_per_10s_deg`, `diagnostics/gait_diagnostic.py`), measuring a new reward term's cost against the *actual* target behavior before choosing a weight (ADR-005/009/010), and warm-start fine-tuning (`training/train.py --init-from`) as the preferred way to introduce a new reward term without risking a from-scratch exploit. `results/compare.py` tabulates all of this for every evaluated run in one command.
- **New capability (2026-09-20, EXP-0032): reward-term annealing within a single training run.** `config.loader.RewardScheduleTerm`/`Config.reward_schedule`, `envs.g1_walk_env.G1WalkEnv.set_curriculum_progress`, and `training.curriculum_callback.CurriculumProgressCallback` let a reward weight ramp linearly from a start value to an end value over a configurable fraction of training, instead of being constant from step 0 or requiring a separate fine-tuning run. Opt-in per config (empty `reward_schedule` = old behavior, verified unchanged by `tests/test_reward_schedule.py`, 6 new tests, 65/65 total passing). First use: testing whether this avoids EXP-0022/0024's from-scratch collapse without needing a separate fine-tuning stage at all -- see `experiments/EXPERIMENT_LOG.md` EXP-0032.

Fine-tuning the established stepping-quality recipe onto a *better* base gait (`exp0023`'s low-LR-from-scratch checkpoint, `dist_ratio=0.848`) instead of onto `exp0011` (`dist_ratio=0.671`) produced a checkpoint (`exp0026`) that beats or matches every prior checkpoint on nearly every axis simultaneously. One narrow gap remains: `exp0019`'s controlled-heading measurement (-7.5 deg/10s) is still better than the new canonical's (-14.1 deg/10s) -- an honestly-reported, minor residual, not a trade-off requiring a choice between checkpoints.
  - Increased PD gains was tested via torque-saturation measurement (2026-09-19) on the old `exp0017`-lineage checkpoints and ruled out (no checkpoint gets within 37% of any joint's torque limit) -- moot now that the surface itself is resolved, but kept for the record since it's still true.
- **High-speed-command falls, re-verified and resolved (2026-09-19, no new training -- existing eval data re-analyzed)**: the 3 seeds that used to fall in every prior baseline (1001/1013/1017, the fastest 3 of 20) now all survive the full 20s on the (then-)canonical checkpoint, confirming 0/20 falls holds at the seed level too. However, the failure mode shifted rather than vanished: those same 3 seeds have the worst `distance_ratio` and largest yaw drift of the run, i.e. the robot survives but walks a curve at high commanded speed instead of falling. This was measured against `exp0017`; re-verifying against the new canonical `exp0026` is a natural, not-yet-done follow-up. - ~~The domain-randomization variant (EXP-0015) was trained on top of the pre-EXP-0016 reward~~ -- done 2026-09-19 (EXP-0021): re-ran the same DR recipe on top of the canonical reward via warm-start fine-tuning, confirming the same robustness/precision trade-off pattern on the better base gait. `exp0021_dr_on_canonical` supersedes `exp0015` for this purpose; see `docs/DECISIONS.md` ADR-006 second resolution.
- Found and fixed one real bug pre-training: `control/joint_map.py`'s motor-vs-position actuator check used `actuator_gaintype` (which does not distinguish them) instead of `actuator_biastype` (which does) -- see `docs/FAILURE_ANALYSIS.md` F-0001. - Every from-scratch PPO run in this project shows a high-`approx_kl`/high-`clip_fraction`, climb-crash-recover oscillation rather than smooth convergence -- fine-tuning runs at low LR notably did NOT show this, suggesting (not yet proven as universal) the oscillation may be a consequence of the learning rate needed for random-init training rather than an unavoidable property of this task.
